Which Places?

What's new

Which Places? is free and open source. These are the updates, newest first. Press Ctrl+Shift+R (⌘⇧R on a Mac) if the site doesn't show the latest version.

Release 1.3 November 2026

  • Neighbourhoods: every one of the UK's 46,844 small areas (LSOAs in England and Wales, data zones in Scotland and Northern Ireland). Open a council, or zoom right in, and the map breaks into neighbourhoods.
  • Postcode finder: type a postcode in the search box to go straight to its neighbourhood.
  • Each neighbourhood has its own page: deprivation (overall, and for income, employment, health and education), population and age, and census figures, compared with its council, nation and the UK.
  • Shade the neighbourhoods by deprivation, age or any census measure, from the key at the top right of the map.
  • Each council's page shows how many of its neighbourhoods are among the most deprived in its nation, and lists the most deprived.
  • Downloads for bids: every neighbourhood in a council, and every figure for a nation, region or city region, as a spreadsheet (CSV).
  • The Charts view no longer shows tall coloured lines at the right-hand edge (they joined each line to its label).

Release 1.2 November 2026

  • Census: 25 census measures for all four nations, including ethnic group, born abroad, religion, English language, health, disability, unpaid carers, cars, housing tenure, overcrowding, older people living alone, lone parents, qualifications and veterans. (England, Wales and Northern Ireland 2021; Scotland 2022.)
  • City regions: 23 city regions (England's combined authorities, Greater London, and the city regions of Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland) can be chosen from the Go to menu, from each area's page, or as a filter.
  • Choosing a nation, region or city region now opens its own page: headline figures for the whole place, compared with its nation and the UK, and every measure in one table, followed by its councils.
  • Figures for a whole place are official where ONS publishes them; otherwise they are worked out from its councils, and estimates are marked ≈.
  • The map no longer shifts when the key changes size.

Release 1.1 November 2026

  • 54 new measures for every area: household income, pay, child poverty, work and benefits, house prices, life expectancy, wellbeing, qualifications and school results, broadband and mobile, services, emissions, and deprivation for all four nations.
  • Each area page now opens with eight headline figures, including household income, child poverty, life expectancy and deprivation, each compared with the region, nation and UK.
  • Measures made differently in each nation (deprivation, new homes, apprenticeships) are compared only within the nation.
  • A bigger map: it now uses the full height of the screen, with the title, search and tabs moved into the panel.
  • London is drawn again, enlarged, in its own box, so every borough can be seen and clicked.
  • Orkney and Shetland share one box in the North Sea, so the UK is drawn larger.
  • A Whole UK button on the map takes you back to the whole country.
  • "Add a filter" shows every topic as tabs.

Release 1.0.3 November 2026

  • New releases now show straight away: the page always checks for a newer version before using the copy saved in your browser.

Release 1.0.2 November 2026

  • A cleaner layout on laptops and big screens: the map on the left at full height, and one panel on the right for filters and results.
  • The five views are tabs along the top bar, next to Statistics, Download, Share and a More menu.
  • A trail at the top of the panel (United Kingdom › nation › region › area) with previous and next buttons, so it is easy to move between areas.
  • Each area opens with headline cards comparing it with its region, nation and the UK median, plus neighbouring and similar areas.
  • The results are a sortable table; pick which measure shades the map.
  • Choosing an area zooms to it and its neighbours. Search now finds places as well as measures.
  • Shetland's inset box has moved to the top-left, so the UK is drawn larger.

Release 1.0.1 November 2026

  • A new layout for a tall country: on a laptop or bigger, the map now fills the left of the screen at full height, with the filters, views and results stacked on the right. Phones keep their own layout.
  • Sharper coastlines: the map now uses ONS's detailed (BGC) boundaries, so coasts and islands look right when you zoom in, and each area's size is more accurate.
  • Shetland in a box: the Shetland Islands are drawn closer to the mainland, as on Ordnance Survey maps, so the rest of the UK can be drawn bigger.
  • Go to: zoom straight to any nation or English region, including London, from the bar above the map.

Release 1.0 November 2026

  • The map: all 361 local authorities of England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, grouped into the 12 UK regions and the four nations. Search for any place by name.
  • Population and age: population, density, growth, median age and the shares of children, working-age adults and over-65s, every year from 1991 (2001 in Northern Ireland) to 2025, with a population pyramid for every area.
  • Ways to look at it: stack filters to narrow the map, then Compare, Rankings, Charts and Insights, the statistics toolkit, About this data, downloads, share links and embedding, all carried over from Which Countries?.
  • Coming next: small areas (neighbourhoods), the census, deprivation, facilities and an area report. See the plan in the project's notes.
