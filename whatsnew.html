Which Places?

What's new

Which Places? is free and open source. These are the updates, newest first. Press Ctrl+Shift+R (⌘⇧R on a Mac) if the site doesn't show the latest version.

Release 1.0.2 November 2026

  • A cleaner layout on laptops and big screens: the map on the left at full height, and one panel on the right for filters and results.
  • The five views are tabs along the top bar, next to Statistics, Download, Share and a More menu.
  • A trail at the top of the panel (United Kingdom › nation › region › area) with previous and next buttons, so it is easy to move between areas.
  • Each area opens with headline cards comparing it with its region, nation and the UK median, plus neighbouring and similar areas.
  • The results are a sortable table; pick which measure shades the map.
  • Choosing an area zooms to it and its neighbours. Search now finds places as well as measures.
  • Shetland's inset box has moved to the top-left, so the UK is drawn larger.

Release 1.0.1 November 2026

  • A new layout for a tall country: on a laptop or bigger, the map now fills the left of the screen at full height, with the filters, views and results stacked on the right. Phones keep their own layout.
  • Sharper coastlines: the map now uses ONS's detailed (BGC) boundaries, so coasts and islands look right when you zoom in, and each area's size is more accurate.
  • Shetland in a box: the Shetland Islands are drawn closer to the mainland, as on Ordnance Survey maps, so the rest of the UK can be drawn bigger.
  • Go to: zoom straight to any nation or English region, including London, from the bar above the map.

Release 1.0 November 2026

  • The map: all 361 local authorities of England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, grouped into the 12 UK regions and the four nations. Search for any place by name.
  • Population and age: population, density, growth, median age and the shares of children, working-age adults and over-65s, every year from 1991 (2001 in Northern Ireland) to 2025, with a population pyramid for every area.
  • Ways to look at it: stack filters to narrow the map, then Compare, Rankings, Charts and Insights, the statistics toolkit, About this data, downloads, share links and embedding, all carried over from Which Countries?.
  • Coming next: small areas (neighbourhoods), the census, deprivation, facilities and an area report. See the plan in the project's notes.
