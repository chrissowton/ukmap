Which Places?

Using Which Places? for a funding bid

Funders want to know who lives in the place you work, what they need, and who else is there. Which Places? puts the official figures in one place. This guide takes about ten minutes.

1. Find your place

  1. Type a postcode, a town or a council in the search box at the top, or use the place menu: first choose the kind of place (nation, region, town or city, city region, council), then the place.
  2. If you work in a few neighbourhoods rather than a whole council, open the council, press ✏️ Make your own area, and click the neighbourhoods you serve. Give it a name and save it. Its figures are added up for you.

2. Describe the need

  1. The cards at the top of each page compare the place with its region, nation and the UK: household income, child poverty, claimants, life expectancy, deprivation.
  2. For neighbourhood-level detail, look at the Neighbourhoods box: how many of its neighbourhoods are among the most deprived 10% in its nation, and which.
  3. Census figures (disability, unpaid carers, no car, overcrowding, English language and more) are under the Census topic.
  4. Choose a measure and the map shows where it is highest. The table can be sorted and downloaded.
Tip: quote the figure, the year and the source, for example “30% of children in Leeds live in relative poverty after housing costs (DWP, 2024/25)”. Every figure's source is under “Sources & methods”, and in the downloads.

3. Compare

  1. Press + Compare to add up to six places side by side: your area, its council, a similar town, the nation.
  2. Download the comparison as a spreadsheet (CSV) for your appendix.

4. Show who else is there

  1. On a council's page, the Charities and schools box lists registered charities based there, and (in England and Wales) charities based elsewhere that work there. Filter by what they do and who they help: useful for finding partners and showing you are not duplicating others.
  2. Tick Charities or Schools under “Show on the map” to see where they are.

5. Print or save

  1. Press 🖨 Print report for a clean summary of any place, then “Print or save as PDF”.
  2. Use the 📝 Notebook to jot down the figures you want to use as you go. It stays in your browser, and you can print or download it.

Good practice

  • Deprivation is measured differently in each nation: compare places within one nation only.
  • Figures marked ≈ are worked out from smaller areas: say “about”.
  • Small-area population figures are estimates: round them to the nearest 100.
  • Charities are counted where they are registered, so head offices push some areas up.
